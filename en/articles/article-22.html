Invitation letter: what it should include

Last reviewed: May 27, 2026 | General information only, not legal advice.

A good invitation letter is short, factual, and aligned with the rest of the file. It should support the purpose of the visit without exaggeration.

  • Invitation letter
  • Tourist visa
  • Entry permits
  • Ashdod, Israel

Key takeaways

  • Keep the letter short and factual.
  • Match dates with travel tickets and lodging.
  • Explain the relationship clearly.
  • Avoid exaggerated promises or statements.

Overview

An invitation letter should include who is inviting, who is visiting, the relationship, dates, and accommodation. The tourist visa page provides broader context.

If the host is providing support, be clear and realistic. Overstated promises can raise questions.

Ensure the letter aligns with the visitor documents and travel plan so the story is consistent.

Some hosts add personal promises that cannot be proven, which can create doubt. Keep the letter factual and limited to what you can show. If you are offering accommodation, include the general location and contact details. Avoid sharing unnecessary personal data. A short letter that matches the rest of the file is usually more persuasive than a long narrative.

Practical checklist

A clean invitation letter should include:

  • Full names and contact details for host and visitor.
  • Purpose of visit and expected dates.
  • Accommodation details in Israel.
  • Relationship description in simple terms.
  • Signature and date.

Common pitfalls

Common letter mistakes:

  • Dates that do not match the travel plan.
  • Unverifiable promises of financial support.
  • Missing contact details for the host.
  • Long narrative that adds confusion.

Align the letter with the file

Check the letter against the visitor itinerary and accommodation details before submission.
